Leaf curl virus is one possible cause when leaves are reduced in size and curl upward,
veins or leaves yellow, internodes shorten, shoots become excessive, plants are dwarfed,
or pods remain small. The Department of Agriculture identifies whitefly as a vector.

Do not diagnose leaf curl virus from "curling leaves" alone. Ask whether curling is upward,
whether veins are yellow, whether the plant is stunted, whether small white insects are
present under leaves, and whether symptoms occur in patches or across the field. Other
causes can resemble curling.

Source-backed non-chemical measures include removing alternative host weeds, using timely
cultivation practices, and establishing suitable wind barriers. A farmer should consult an
agricultural instructor when symptoms are spreading or identification remains uncertain.
