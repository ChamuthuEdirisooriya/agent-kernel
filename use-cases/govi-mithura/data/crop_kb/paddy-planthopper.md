High planthopper populations can cause leaves to turn orange-yellow and then brown and dry,
producing patches called hopperburn. To distinguish this from other damage, inspect near the
base of tillers for white-to-brown nymphs or adults and for honeydew or sooty mould. Similar
field browning can have other causes, so insect presence matters.

IRRI recommends regular inspection near stems and the water surface, weed removal, avoiding
indiscriminate insecticide use that destroys natural enemies, and using locally recommended
resistant varieties. Do not reproduce numeric intervention thresholds or prescribe an
insecticide; consult the local agriculture office when populations or drying patches rise.
