Bacterial wilt is one possibility when one or several plants in a zone wilt, young leaves
wilt during the hottest part of the day, wilting becomes permanent, and affected plants die
within a few days. Department of Agriculture guidance describes bacterial ooze from a clean
cut stem suspended in clear water as a field indication, but the agent must not represent
this as laboratory confirmation.

Source-backed measures include removing infected plants, roots, surrounding affected soil,
and host weeds; improving drainage; rotating with non-host crops; and preventing water from
an infected field from reaching a healthy field. Recommend prompt local agricultural advice
when wilting is spreading.
